# Hackathon brief

The official brief for the AI-Native Startup Hackathon, condensed from the
organizer's document on October 7, 2026. Submit at
https://hackathons.crewbasecollective.com

## The ask

Build a startup run by AI agents. The agents form the founding team: they
research, plan, build, sell, operate and make decisions. By the end, the AI
team has built something real and pitches it to investors, answering their
questions live.

The investor conversation should feel like a real fundraising meeting, not a
product demo.

## Rules

- Solo or a team of 2.
- Any industry, problem, customer or business model.
- A minimum of 5 AI employee agents.
- You decide the agents' roles, personalities, responsibilities and how they
  work together.

## Phases

| | Phase 1, build day | Phase 2, October 7-11 |
|---|---|---|
| Where | In person, 10:00 AM to 6:00 PM | Online and in person |
| Agents | 3 to 5 | 5 or more |
| Goal | Idea, validation, simple MVP, initial AI team, demo | Working AI organization, users, traction, investor pitch |

Phase 1 must be completed to continue to Phase 2.

Phase 2 work: expand to 5+ agents, connect them into an organization with
real workflows, improve the MVP, launch and test with real users, execute GTM
and sales, collect feedback, build traction, prepare the investor pitch and
Q&A.

## What must exist by the end

| Deliverable | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Company and problem | Name, problem, solution, ideal customer, value proposition |
| Market research | Market size, customer insights, trends, competitors, alternatives |
| Product strategy | Vision, key features, MVP scope, roadmap, differentiation |
| Working MVP | A functional product investors can try |
| Landing page | A live site explaining the company, product, customer, with a call to action |
| GTM and sales | Target customers, channels, launch strategy, sales pipeline |
| Business model | Pricing, revenue model, unit economics |
| Traction and validation | Users, feedback, pilots, signups, revenue or other demand evidence |
| Marketing assets | Social posts, campaigns, ads |
| Financial model | Revenue projections, costs, margins, growth assumptions, funding ask |
| Investor pitch deck | Opportunity, product, market, business, traction, competition, vision |
| AI founding team | 5+ AI employees with clear roles and evidence of the work they did |
| Investor Q&A | Agents answer questions on company, product, market, finances |
| Live investor pitch | The pitch, with AI employees responding to questions in real time |

## Questions the agents must be able to answer

1. What are you building, and what problem are you solving?
2. Who is your ideal customer, and what evidence shows they need this?
3. How big is the market, and how big can this company become?
4. Who are your competitors, and why is your solution better?
5. Why is now the right time for this company?
6. How does your product work, and why will customers choose it?
7. How will you acquire your first 100 customers, and scale beyond that?
8. How does the company make money, and do the unit economics work?
9. What traction or validation do you have so far?
10. What are the biggest risks, and how will you overcome them?
11. What will you achieve with the next round of funding?
12. Why should we invest in this company?

## Suggested agent roles

Market Research, Product Manager, Brand, UX/UI Designer, Software Engineer,
Web Developer, Growth, Sales, Customer Success, Operations, Finance, Data and
Analytics, Content and Marketing, Fundraising, Legal and Compliance, Strategy,
QA and Testing, Automation, PR and Communications, or any custom role.

AdaL is meant to be the primary development and execution platform. The rest
complement it.

Core loop, in their words:

Kylon, BAND, Querit / Apify / Glasser, AdaL, RocketRide, Paritok, Tenki,
Prelint, AdaL, InstaCloud. Voiskey provides the voice interface and Finch adds
skills throughout.

## Their worked examples

All three follow one shape: validate an idea, assemble the AI team, let the
agents research, build and market, produce a real demoable company, prepare
the pitch, defend it live.

- Restaurant food waste: Research, Product, Designer, Engineer and Growth
  agents. In Q&A the Sales and Strategy agents defend the business.
- Recruiting: Research, Product, UX, Engineering, Sales and Finance agents.
  Strategy answers on competition, Finance on unit economics, Product or
  Engineering gives the live demo.
- Developer debugging: seven agents including QA and Marketing. $49 per
  developer per month. Strategy fields "why can't GitHub build this?".

Their RocketRide pipeline examples:

- Collect competitor data, enrich with Glasser, analyze, generate report,
  review, deliver.
- Define ICP, discover leads, enrich with Glasser, research, score,
  prioritize, generate outreach, review, deliver.
